Buying a Refurbished Laptop: What to Check Before You Buy

Created by Jamal Gardezi · October 25, 2026 · For US and Canadian shoppers

A refurbished laptop can be a sensible way to buy a better-built computer without paying the original new price. But a large discount is only useful if the laptop can run your applications, hold enough charge and arrive with clear protection if something is wrong.

Start with the exact model and its condition. Then compare its total cost with similar refurbished options and a suitable new laptop. The checklist below helps you make that comparison.

1. Understand what “refurbished” means

Refurbishment generally involves checking a previously owned or returned device and restoring it to working condition. The work performed varies: a listing may describe cleaning and testing, repairs or replacement parts. Do not assume that every refurbished laptop has a new battery, a new screen or original accessories.

A used laptop is sold in its described used condition. An open-box item may have been unpacked or returned; that label alone does not tell you how much it was used. Ask what was tested and what was replaced.

The US eBay Refurbished program uses Certified, Excellent, Very Good and Good condition tiers. The grade describes the condition you should expect, but you still need to read the individual listing. Ordinary used listings and items described casually as “refurbished” should not be assumed to include the program’s benefits.

“Core i7” or “Ryzen 7” is not enough information. Ask for the full processor model and compare its generation and performance. An older premium processor may be a less useful purchase than a newer midrange option.

Check whether RAM is soldered or replaceable, how much memory the machine supports, and whether the SSD can be upgraded. Confirm the display resolution and panel type: a sharp, comfortable screen matters during a full day of work. For many buyers, a Full HD display is a useful starting target.

Verify operating-system eligibility on the manufacturer’s or OS vendor’s website. “Windows installed” does not by itself establish official hardware support, a valid licence or a long remaining support life.

3. Ask about battery health and the charger

A laptop can work well on a desk and still be disappointing away from a power outlet. Ask the seller for the battery’s full-charge capacity relative to its design capacity, its cycle count if available, and any minimum battery-health commitment.

For frequent travel, you might set a personal target such as at least 80% of design capacity. That is a buying preference, not a universal refurbishment standard or a promise of a particular runtime. Brightness, applications and battery age all affect endurance.

Check that an appropriate charger is included, including its wattage and connector. If you will need a replacement battery or charger, include that cost before deciding whether the deal is good.

4. Read the listing like an inspection report

  • Screen: look for disclosed scratches, pressure marks, dead pixels or uneven brightness.
  • Body: check hinges, corners, ports and signs of damage around the charging socket.
  • Keyboard: confirm the layout, backlight if needed, and whether worn keys were replaced with stickers.
  • Connectivity: verify Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, USB ports, video output and any docking requirements.
  • Ownership locks: ask whether BIOS passwords, activation locks and previous-company device management have been removed.
  • Photos: establish whether images show the actual unit or are representative stock photos.

Prefer a seller who answers specific questions clearly. Review recent feedback about laptops, condition accuracy, packaging and how problems were resolved. A high overall rating is useful context, but it does not replace the listing details.

5. Separate the warranty from the return policy

A return policy explains when and how you can send the laptop back. A warranty explains which faults may be covered after purchase. Check both before paying, including who pays return shipping, exclusions, the claim process and the coverage start date.

eBay’s US program describes one- or two-year warranty coverage, depending on the refurbishment tier. Its warranty page specifies one year for Excellent, Very Good and Good refurbished items. Read the badge, warranty terms and listing rather than assuming every laptop on eBay qualifies.

Canadian buyers should confirm delivery eligibility, currency conversion, import charges, return logistics and whether warranty service is available in Canada. A US listing’s protection should not be assumed to apply identically across borders.

6. Compare the real price, not just the discount

Add the laptop price, shipping, tax, import charges and any necessary accessories or repairs. Compare the same configuration and condition with alternatives available now. A percentage reduction against an old reference price does not prove it is the best current value.

For example, a hypothetical $300 laptop needing a $70 battery and $30 charger costs $400 before tax and shipping. A $380 option with suitable accessories and documented battery health could be the more practical choice. These figures illustrate the calculation; they are not live offers.

7. Test the laptop promptly after delivery

Keep the packaging and order details while checking that the serial number and specifications match the listing. Inspect the screen and hinges, then test the keyboard, trackpad, webcam, microphone, speakers, Wi-Fi and available ports. Confirm the charger works and look at the battery report.

Run the manufacturer’s built-in diagnostics where available. Check for unexpected shutdowns, excessive fan noise and account or organisation-enrolment prompts. Document any mismatch and contact the seller through eBay within the applicable return window. Keep your receipt and warranty information.

Common questions

Is a refurbished laptop suitable for work or study?

It can be, if its specifications, software support, battery condition and seller protections meet your needs. For business use, also check your employer’s security and device-management requirements.

Does refurbished mean the battery is new?

No. Only rely on that claim when the listing explicitly states it. Ask for battery-health information and the seller’s battery policy.

Can a refurbished laptop handle gaming?

Some can. Check the exact GPU and CPU against your games and desired resolution. A “gaming” label alone does not establish performance.

Should I choose the biggest advertised discount?

Compare total price, configuration, condition and protection first. A smaller discount on a better-suited laptop can be the better purchase.
