Read the material description carefully

Gold-plated, gold-filled and solid gold describe different constructions. A gold-plated item has a surface layer of gold over another material. Check the base metal as well as the finish, and read the seller’s material description for posts, chains and clasps separately.

Compare dimensions with something you own

For earrings, check hoop diameter, drop length and item weight. For necklaces, compare chain length and any extender with the neckline you plan to wear. For bracelets, measure your wrist and check the adjustable range or interior dimensions rather than relying on a size label.

Choose the closure and finish for everyday use

Check whether earrings use posts, hinges or hooks, and whether bracelets have a clasp or a stretch band. Read the care instructions before assuming a plated piece is suitable for showering or swimming. Marketing descriptions such as “waterproof” or “hypoallergenic” do not replace the material details.

Make the gift easy to get right

Confirm the selected color, item count, packaging and return conditions. A set may contain several pairs while a different variant contains one. Check the estimated delivery date for the recipient’s ZIP code and whether gift packaging is included.

Common buying questions

Does 14K gold-plated mean solid 14K gold?

No. Gold plating is a surface layer over another material. Check the base metal and finish description separately; do not treat a plated listing as a solid-gold listing.

Which jewelry measurements should I check for a gift?

Check chain length for necklaces, wrist fit for bracelets, and diameter or drop length for earrings. Compare those dimensions with a piece the recipient already wears.
